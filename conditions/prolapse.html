Prolapse

Pelvic organ prolapse occurs when the muscles and connective tissue supporting the pelvic organs weaken, allowing the bladder, uterus or bowel to descend into the vagina. It is very common, particularly after childbirth and with age, and is not dangerous — but it can be uncomfortable and affect quality of life.

Symptoms include a sensation of heaviness, dragging or a bulge in the vagina, difficulty emptying the bladder or bowel, urinary leakage, and discomfort during intercourse. Symptoms often worsen through the day or with standing and lifting.

Assessment involves a gentle examination to identify which organs are involved and to what degree. Bladder function tests are sometimes arranged if urinary symptoms are prominent.

Many women benefit from conservative management — pelvic floor physiotherapy, lifestyle adjustments and, where appropriate, a vaginal pessary. When symptoms are significant and conservative measures have not helped, surgical repair may be discussed. The right approach depends on your symptoms, your general health and your priorities.
